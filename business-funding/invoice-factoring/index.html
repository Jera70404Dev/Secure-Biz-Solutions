How invoice factoring works

With invoice factoring, you sell your unpaid invoices to a factoring company. They advance you up to 90% of the invoice value right away, then collect from your customer. When your customer pays, you receive the rest, minus the factoring fee.

It's built for businesses that bill other businesses: trucking, staffing, manufacturing, wholesale and contractors. Approval depends mostly on your customers' credit, not yours.

Why business owners choose invoice factoring

  • Cash in days: stop waiting on slow-paying customers.
  • Grows with your sales: the more you invoice, the more you can access.
  • Your customers' credit matters most: strong customers can offset weaker personal credit.
  • Not a loan: you're selling an asset, so it doesn't add debt to your books.

Frequently asked questions

How does invoice factoring work?

You sell unpaid invoices, get up to 90% of their value now, and receive the rest, minus a fee, when your customer pays.

How fast do I get paid?

Usually within one to three days of submitting an invoice, once your account is set up.

What does it cost?

Typically a small percentage of the invoice value, depending on how fast your customers pay. Your advisor shows the exact fee before you sign.

Does my credit matter?

Less than with a loan. The factoring company cares most about your customers' ability to pay.

Will my customers know?

Usually, yes, because they pay the factoring company directly. This is common and accepted in many industries.

Do I have to factor every invoice?

Not always. Some programs let you choose which invoices to factor.

What is recourse vs. non-recourse?

With recourse, you cover invoices your customer never pays. With non-recourse, the factor takes that risk for a higher fee.

Which industries use factoring?

Trucking, staffing, manufacturing, wholesale, distribution, government contractors and construction.

What do I need to apply?

A list of your open invoices, your customer list and basic business details.

Is factoring a loan?

No. You're selling an asset, so it doesn't add debt to your balance sheet.
