How a line of credit works

A line of credit gives you a credit limit you can draw from whenever you need cash. You pay interest only on the amount you've drawn, and as you repay, that amount becomes available again.

It's built for ongoing needs: covering payroll between big payments, buying inventory, or handling a surprise repair. Many owners keep one open as a safety net.

Why business owners choose lines of credit

  • Pay only for what you use: no interest on money you haven't drawn.
  • Reusable: repay and draw again without reapplying.
  • Fast access: once open, draws can reach your account in a day or two.
  • A safety net: cover slow months and surprises without scrambling.

Frequently asked questions

How does a line of credit work?

You get a limit, draw what you need, and pay interest only on what you've drawn. Repaid amounts become available again.

How big a line can I get?

From $10,000 up to $500,000, based on your revenue and credit.

How fast can I get approved?

Often one to three business days.

Do I pay anything if I don't use it?

Some lines have a small maintenance or draw fee. Your advisor shows every fee before you choose.

What credit score do I need?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher.

How do I repay?

Each draw is repaid in set weekly or monthly payments, often over 6 to 24 months.

Can I use it for payroll?

Yes. Payroll, inventory and short-term cash gaps are the most common uses.

Is it secured?

Many lines are unsecured. Larger lines may need collateral.

Can my line grow over time?

Often, yes. Lenders may raise your limit after a record of on-time payments.

Should I get a line of credit or a term loan?

A line is best for ongoing or unpredictable needs. A term loan is best for one big purchase.
