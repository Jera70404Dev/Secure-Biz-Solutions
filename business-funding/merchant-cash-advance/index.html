How a merchant cash advance works

A merchant cash advance gives you a lump sum in exchange for a share of your future sales. Instead of fixed monthly payments, you repay a small amount each day or week from your deposits or card sales. This page also covers revenue-based financing, which works the same way.

Approval is based mostly on your revenue and bank deposits, so it's one of the fastest options and works for many owners with credit challenges. It costs more than a traditional loan, so it's best for short-term needs where speed matters.

Why business owners choose merchant cash advance

  • Fast: often funded in one to two business days.
  • Credit-flexible: approval looks mainly at your sales and deposits.
  • Payments follow your revenue: with many programs, slower weeks mean smaller payments.
  • No collateral required: you don't pledge equipment or property.

Frequently asked questions

Is a merchant cash advance a loan?

No. It's a purchase of a share of your future sales. You repay from your revenue instead of fixed loan payments.

How fast can I get funded?

Often one to two business days after approval.

What credit score do I need?

Many programs work with scores of 500 and up, because approval is based mainly on your revenue.

How is it repaid?

A set daily or weekly amount, or a percentage of your card sales, taken automatically.

How much does it cost?

The cost is shown as a factor rate, a fixed amount you repay on top of what you receive. Your advisor shows the total payback for every offer before you choose.

How much can I get?

From $5,000 up to $500,000, usually based on your monthly revenue.

Can I get one if I already have an advance?

Sometimes. Your advisor will review what you already owe and tell you honestly whether more funding makes sense.

What is revenue-based financing?

A close cousin of the cash advance: you repay a percentage of your revenue until a set total is paid.

What do I need to apply?

Your three most recent business bank statements and basic business details.

When does a cash advance make sense?

For short-term needs where speed matters more than cost, like a time-sensitive inventory deal or an urgent repair.
